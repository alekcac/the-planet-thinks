Watch Wikipedia edits live: every tool, tested

Checked by hand on 26 August 2026 — each one opened in a browser and watched.

Four tools still show Wikipedia being edited in real time: Listen to Wikipedia turns edits into sound, The Planet Thinks draws them on a globe, Wikipedia Live has an AI narrate them, and overwatch.earth puts them on a globe alongside flights and ships. Two well-known ones — the Wikipedia Recent Changes Map and WikiMap — no longer show any edits, for a reason worth knowing.

Why half the maps went dark

The older tools mapped the editor. When someone edited Wikipedia without logging in, their IP address was published alongside the edit, and a map could look up roughly where that address was. That is what made a "map of Wikipedia editors" possible at all.

In November 2025 Wikipedia moved unregistered editors to temporary accounts. Anonymous edits are now credited to a short-lived account name instead of an IP address — a clear privacy improvement, and the end of the road for anything built on those IPs. Hatnote's Recent Changes Map says so plainly in its own sunset notice.

The tools that survived never looked at editors in the first place. They place an edit by the location of the thing being edited: if someone improves the article about a river, the river lights up. That data comes from the article's own coordinates, so no privacy change can take it away.

Listen to Wikipedia

The original, and still the one most people mean. Bells for additions, plucked strings for removals, pitch set by the size of the edit; circles bloom and fade over a white field. It is not a map and never tried to be — it is the ambient one, and it has aged beautifully. When we checked it, it was running at 17 edits a minute.

The Planet Thinks

Ours, so weigh this accordingly. A realistic 3D Earth with a moving day/night line; every edit to an article that has map coordinates lights up at that place, in the colour of its language edition. Click a spark and you get the article and the actual diff. About a fifth of Wikipedia carries coordinates, so the rest of the traffic is counted in the header rather than drawn. There is also a photo mode for live Wikimedia Commons uploads, a full-screen mode for a TV or second monitor, and a daily digest. Free, no sign-up, no tracking cookies, MIT-licensed.

Wikipedia Live

The newest idea of the group, and a genuinely different one: instead of drawing the edits it reports on them. Every 45 seconds an AI model reads the last window of activity and writes a short bulletin — how much of it is bots, which articles drew a revert, which rename was quietly funny. If you want to know what the edit stream means rather than watch it move, this is the one. It is text, not a globe, despite occasionally being described as one.

overwatch.earth

A single 3D globe carrying roughly 34 live feeds — flights, ships, weather, satellites, internet events — with Wikipedia edits as one layer among them. If you want one screen for everything happening on Earth rather than one subject watched closely, it covers far more ground than anything else here. A consent screen loads before the globe does; we did not pass it, so this description follows the site's own account of itself.

Recent Changes Map, WikiMap, Wikistream

Recent Changes Map has been formally retired and says so on its front page, pointing visitors to Listen to Wikipedia. WikiMap still loads its world map and its counters, but after a full minute of watching it reported zero edits received. Wikistream draws its filter bar and then nothing at all. All three are worth remembering: for years they were how people saw this.

Frequently asked

What are some websites like Listen to Wikipedia? The four working tools above. If you want the same ambient quality in a different sense, the globe versions are closest; if you want words, Wikipedia Live is the one.

Can you still see where Wikipedia editors are? No, and that is the point. Since November 2025 anonymous edits are credited to temporary accounts rather than IP addresses, so editor locations are no longer public. Live maps today show where the subject is.

Is there a live map of Wikipedia edits? Yes — this site is one, and overwatch.earth carries the same feed on its multi-feed globe.

Where does the data come from? All of these read the same public source: Wikimedia EventStreams, which publishes every saved change within about a second. Nobody here has special access; the difference is only in what each one does with it.

And the same thing for OpenStreetMap

Wikipedia is not the only map-shaped project being edited around the clock. If watching edits land is the appeal, the same globe carries OpenStreetMap, where every spark is a volunteer's editing session; the OpenStreetMap-only tools in that space are Show Me The Way and live.openstreetmap.fr.
